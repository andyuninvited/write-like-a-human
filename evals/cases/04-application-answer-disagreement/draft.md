At my previous company, leadership wanted to launch a referral program to accelerate growth. While I understood the goal, I believed the timing was wrong. The real problem was that our delivery experience was inconsistent, and referrals would amplify that.

Rather than simply voicing concerns, I decided to utilize data to make the case. I pulled three months of customer complaints and built a robust analysis showing that late deliveries were the top driver of churn. I presented this to leadership in a way designed to foster alignment and open dialogue.

As a result, the team agreed to delay the launch by three weeks while operations addressed the delivery issues. When we did launch, referral signups came in 30% above forecast.

Ultimately, this experience taught me that disagreement, when handled with data and empathy, can lead to better outcomes for everyone.
